The Self-Employed Tax Deduction Checklist

Every legitimate write-off lowers both your income tax and your 15.3% self-employment tax. Here's what freelancers and small business owners can deduct in 2026.

Vehicle & travel

  • Business mileage at $0.725/mile (or actual vehicle expenses)
  • Parking and tolls on business trips
  • Airfare, lodging, and 50% of meals for business travel

Workspace

  • Home office — the dedicated-use portion of rent/mortgage interest, utilities, and insurance (or the simplified $5/sq ft method)
  • Coworking memberships and office rent
  • Office furniture and improvements

Equipment & supplies

  • Computers, cameras, tools, and machinery (often fully deductible in year one via Section 179 or bonus depreciation; larger assets depreciate on a MACRS schedule)
  • Software subscriptions and app services
  • Office supplies and shipping

Services & fees

  • Contractors and freelancers you hire (file 1099s!)
  • Accountant, bookkeeper, and legal fees
  • Bank fees, payment processing fees, and business insurance
  • Advertising, website hosting, and marketing

The big personal-adjacent ones people miss

  • Self-employed health insurance premiums (for you and your family)
  • Retirement contributions — SEP-IRA or Solo 401(k)
  • Half of your self-employment tax
  • Business portion of your phone and internet
  • Professional education, courses, and industry subscriptions

The rule that makes deductions survive an audit

A deduction is only as good as its record. For each expense you want: the date, the amount, the vendor, and the business purpose — ideally with the receipt attached. Categorizing expenses as they happen beats reconstructing a year every April, and it also makes your quarterly estimated payments accurate instead of guesses.
